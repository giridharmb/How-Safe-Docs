# How Safe

**A community map of how safe places feel, by day and after dark, for everyone.**

How Safe is a free iPhone app. People rate how safe a street, park, station or parking lot feels, and
the map turns those ratings into colours: **red** where people feel unsafe, **amber** where they say to
stay alert, and **green** where they feel comfortable. The more people agree, the stronger the colour.

It's made for **everyone**: a woman walking home from a late shift, a man new to a city, a student
heading to the station, a parent choosing a park, a traveller picking a hotel neighbourhood.

---

## What you can do

### See the map
- Apple Maps with your location and a colour overlay of community safety ratings.
- Zoom in down to individual blocks (about 40 m) or out to whole neighbourhoods.
- Colour strength shows **confidence**: one rating gives a faint tint, dozens of ratings give a solid colour.
- Search any address or place, and switch between standard and satellite views.

### Rate any place
Tap anywhere on the map, or press **Rate here**, and choose one of five levels:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟥 Very unsafe | I felt in danger or something happened here |
| 🟧 Unsafe | I felt uncomfortable and would avoid it |
| 🟨 Use caution | Okay, but stay alert |
| 🟩 Safe | I felt comfortable here |
| 🟩 Very safe | Busy, well lit, I'd recommend it |

Then add as much or as little detail as you like:
- **When:** morning, afternoon, evening, night or late night, and weekday or weekend
- **Place type:** street, park, bus or tram stop, train or metro station, parking lot, nightlife, shops, campus, trail, ATM, residential
- **Concerns:** harassment, catcalling, being followed, theft, assault, aggressive begging, drug activity, drunk crowds, poor lighting, isolated, traffic danger, unsafe for LGBTQ+, vandalism, stray dogs
- **Good things:** well lit, busy, CCTV, security guards, police nearby, women-friendly, family-friendly, LGBTQ+ friendly, wheelchair accessible, shops open late
- **Context:** alone or with others, and your own experience or something you heard
- **A short note** (optional, up to 500 characters)

### Read an area
Tap a coloured area to see:
- The overall level and how many people rated it
- How opinions are spread across the five levels
- **Daytime and after-dark scores** shown separately
- The most-reported concerns and positives
- Written notes, with the most helpful first

### Help the community
- Mark notes **helpful** or **not helpful**. Helpful ratings count a bit more in the score.
- **Report** notes that include personal information, hate, spam or false claims. Notes reported by several people are hidden automatically.

### Filter for what matters to you
- **Time:** any time, daytime, after dark, or "right now" (the current time of day)
- **Rated by:** women, men or non-binary people, counting only those who chose to share their gender
- **Recency:** the last 30 days, 6 months, year, or all time
- **Concerns:** for example, show only areas where people mentioned poor lighting
- **First-hand experiences only**, and **hide areas with very few ratings**

### Share and navigate
- Share an area with friends as a link plus a short summary ("Rated *Unsafe* by 12 people, reported: poor lighting, isolated").
- Open any spot in **Apple Maps** or **Google Maps**.

### SOS
- Call your local emergency number in one tap (after a confirmation step)
- Share your current location
- Find the nearest **police stations, hospitals, fire stations and pharmacies**, with walking directions

## Privacy first
- **No sign-up, no ads, no trackers.**
- Your profile (gender, age range, how you get around) is **optional** and **stays on your phone**. It's attached to your ratings, anonymously, only if you turn that on.
- Pins are rounded to about 11 m, and your location history is never stored.
- Delete everything you've posted at any time from Settings.

Read the full [privacy policy](privacy-policy/).

## Important
How Safe shows **people's impressions, not official crime statistics**. Areas change, and one
person's experience isn't everyone's. Always trust your own judgement, and **in an emergency, call
your local emergency number**.
