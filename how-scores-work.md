# How scores work

We're open about how the map is coloured, so you can judge for yourself how much to rely on it.

1. **Levels become numbers.** Very unsafe −2 · Unsafe −1 · Use caution 0 · Safe +1 · Very safe +2
2. **Ratings are grouped into map cells.** Cell size changes with zoom: about 5 km, then 1.2 km, then 150 m, then 40 m.
3. **One person, one voice.** Only each person's most recent rating in a cell counts. Rating again replaces the old rating instead of adding to it.
4. **Recent ratings matter more.** A rating loses half its weight every 6 months, so places can improve or get worse over time.
5. **First-hand counts more.** Second-hand ("heard from someone") ratings carry half the weight.
6. **The community helps.** Each net "helpful" vote adds 10 % weight (up to double). Each net "not helpful" vote removes 15 % (down to 20 %).
7. **Problem content is left out.** A rating reported by 3 or more people, or one voted down heavily, isn't counted.
8. **The score** is the weighted average. Red is around −2, amber around 0, green around +2.
9. **Confidence** depends on how many recent, independent ratings a cell has. Few ratings give a faint colour; many give a strong colour. You can hide low-confidence areas in Filters.

**Filters change the score.** For example, choosing *After dark* and *Rated by women* recalculates every cell using only those ratings.
